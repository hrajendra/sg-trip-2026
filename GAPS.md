## 😴 Jet Lag & Sleep (US family)

*SFO → Singapore is +15 hours — a full day inversion. Grandparents (BLR→SIN) are +2.5 hours — mild adjustment.*

**Night-by-night risk:**
- Night 1 (Apr 6): Bodies think it's 3 AM SFO — crash easily. Actually fine.
- Night 2 (Apr 7): Kids wake 1–3 AM SIN. Hard day ahead (Day 3 is the longest).
- Night 3 (Apr 8): Wakes 3–5 AM SIN. Getting better.
- Night 4 (Apr 9): Wakes 5–6 AM SIN. Nearly adjusted.

39. ✅ **Pre-trip sleep shift** — Start 2 weeks before departure. Shift kids' bedtime 15 min earlier every 2 days. Target: 1–1.5 hrs ahead of normal SFO schedule by day of flight.

40. ✅ **In-flight sleep strategy** — Keep kids awake first half of flight (daytime SIN), sleep second half (night SIN). Snacks timed to Singapore meal times.

41. ✅ **Night 2 wake-up plan** — When kids are up at 2 AM before Day 3 (the hardest day): no screens, minimal stimulation, quiet activity, back to sleep. Need a specific plan documented.

42. ✅ **Day 3 scheduling risk post jet lag** — Starting Day 3 at 8:15 AM after likely broken Night 2, ending at 10 PM. The most demanding day of the trip falls at peak jet lag. Consider whether Day 3's morning start can be pushed to 9 AM.

43. ✅ **Toddler early-wake kit** — Quiet activity bag in hotel room for 3 AM starts: sticker books, small Duplo, no lights or noise. Pack before departure.

44. ✅ **White noise app** — Helps kids stay asleep in unfamiliar environment. Install and test before travel.

45. ✅ **No post-4 PM naps rule** — Especially Days 1–3. However tempting after broken nights, late naps push night sleep later and worsen jet lag.

## 🔄 Guided Mode Prompt — Updates Needed

Current Phase 1 doesn't ask about:
- Group home timezone and flight direction
- Children's ages and current sleep schedules
- Pre-trip preparation window (how many weeks before departure)
- Prior experience with international travel + young children
- Travel insurance status
- Re-entry requirements (OCI, visa) for all passports

Phase 2 (app) should include:
- Jet lag recovery notes in each day's wellness card
- Weather acclimatisation framing in Info tab
- Explicit hydration + cooling break notes in schedule
- **Scrutinise the draft plan with the user before finalising** — ask: does the longest day fall at peak jet lag? Are there enough cooling breaks? Is every person's needs accounted for at every meal?

53. **Keep the user engaged through progressive build-and-refine cycles, not exhaustive upfront questioning.** Whether prompt-based, form-based, or guided — the interaction should feel like building with a collaborator, not filling out a visa application. Key principles:
    - **Start with the minimum viable set of questions** — destination, dates, group size, one or two key constraints. That's enough to produce a first draft.
    - **Build something early** — show a rough Day 1 after 5 questions, not after 50. Momentum matters. A user who sees something tangible in the first 10 minutes stays engaged; one who answers 30 questions before seeing anything drops off.
    - **Get feedback before going deeper** — "Here's a rough Day 1 based on what you've told me. Does this feel right? Too packed? Anyone I haven't accounted for yet?" is more useful than asking all the dietary questions upfront.
    - **Scrutinise and surface gaps progressively** — after each draft, Claude identifies what it doesn't know yet and asks the one most important missing question, not ten at once.
    - **Iterate to specificity** — first pass is a skeleton, second pass adds food and logistics, third pass adds person-specific notes, fourth pass stress-tests jet lag and pacing. Each pass is short, visible, and gives the user something to react to.
    - **Suggestions, not just questions** — Claude should proactively flag risks ("your longest day falls on peak jet lag night — want to adjust?") rather than waiting for the user to think of everything.
    - The goal: user feels like they're co-designing a great trip in real time, not being interviewed before the real work begins.
